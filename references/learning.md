# Learning the user — quick guide

The goal: a vault where the user recognizes themselves. Ask questions one at a
time in natural conversation. Capture each answer as it comes. Stop asking when
`00_self/` is filled and the user has moved on.

## What to learn, in order

1. **Identity** — name, what they do, tools/stack, context. (identity.md)
2. **Mission** — why they build what they build. (mission.md)
3. **Priorities** — what matters right now. This changes often. (priorities.md)
4. **Voice** — how their writing sounds. Best from real examples: have them
   paste something they wrote, or the agent writes a sample and gets corrected.
   (voice.md)
5. **Rules** — hard rules for the agent. These are non-negotiable. (rules.md)
6. **Projects** — what they're actively working on. (10_projects/)
7. **Knowledge** — what they know deeply, so the agent never re-explains it.
   (30_knowledge/)

## Conversation starters

- "What should I call you?"
- "What are you building right now?"
- "What's the one thing that matters most this week?"
- "How do you want your writing to sound? Paste an example."
- "What should I never do?"
- "What do you know well enough that I should just learn from you?"

## Capturing corrections

Corrections are the highest-value data. When the user says "no, make it
shorter" or "always do X", write it into the right note immediately — it's
their brain telling you how to work with them.

## Stop conditions

- The `00_self/` notes are filled and the user has pivoted to real work.
- The user says they don't want to answer right now. Never force it.
- Later sessions: only ask when a note is clearly missing, not as a ritual.
